      Combat rules

      STR increases damage. SPD increases movement, attack rate and dodge. DUR increases health and armor. IQ improves accuracy, critical hits, leading shots and combat decisions. Fighters strafe, pursue, evade incoming attacks and steer away from walls. Melee swings have wind-up and recovery; ranged fighters try to retreat and maintain firing distance. MAG fuels spells and their strength. Stat scaling uses square roots so each stat matters without making characters or weapons larger.

      Weapons set reach, cadence and melee, ranged or arcane attacks. Each ability has its own effect: spells use mana, techniques use stamina. Class and subclass favor fitting weapons and abilities. Weaknesses affect specific damage types, casting, movement, recovery or arena conditions. Custom power names release an arcane bolt.

      Random time, weather and ground are chosen once per game, shared by both fighters, and kept for replay. Two neutral wisps can be hit by attacks; innocent-protecting fighters avoid endangering them. Corner rations restore nutrition and a little mana. The central relic is active at dusk and night. Arena conditions affect combat rather than the original scored traits.

      Formats & time limit

      A game lasts up to 90 simulated seconds. At the limit, remaining health percentage wins, then damage dealt, then a seeded coin toss. Bo1 is first to one win, Bo3 first to two, and Bo5 first to three. A series ends as soon as a fighter reaches the required wins. Single- and double-elimination grand finals and bracket resets are always Bo5. Each game starts at full health and mana.

      Cups

      Style uses the rolled weapon. Filters apply only to tournament entry; each combination keeps its own current champion.

      Progress through league results: promotions and relegations replace new stat rewards. Previously earned fighter bonuses remain.

      Simulation saves after each completed stage. Partial progress stays in this tab; use Save progress before reloading if you stop early.

      Stages

      Suggestions aim for groups of four and a final bracket of up to 16. Swiss qualifies every fighter reaching three wins and eliminates fighters reaching three losses, over at most five rounds. Swiss byes count as wins; a final Swiss stage adds a championship bracket.

      Stages run in order. Group matches alternate across groups within each round. Swiss advances every three-win fighter. Groups use win 3, draw 1, loss 0, and advance by group placement then points per match; brackets use elimination order. Timed-out group games draw; Swiss and knockout games resolve a winner. Ties use game difference, opponents’ points, then initial order. No fixed entrant cap; larger fields take longer to simulate. Choose Bo1, Bo3 or Bo5 per stage; grand finals are always Bo5. Swiss avoids rematches when possible; an odd field receives rotating byes.

      Leagues

      Seven leagues play a full season, then seven division cups and two interleague cups, before an explicit rollover starts the next season. The complete rules and saving behavior are under Rules & saving at the bottom of the Leagues screen.

      Team battles (2v2, 3v3 & 5v5)

      Every fighter is on the field at once. Each fighter gets a role from its class, abilities, weapon and stats: ⛨ Tank, ✚ Healer, ◎ Controller or ✦ Damage. Fighters pick targets by distance, wounds, role and who is threatening their teammates.

      • Tanks take 35% less damage (Fortified), draw nearby enemies (Provoke) and absorb a quarter of the damage dealt to allies close to them (Bulwark).
      • Healers stay behind the frontline; Healing touch, Regeneration, Force fields and Spirit armor can land on teammates, and Life creation is placed where injured allies gather.
      • Controllers lock down the enemies threatening their carries; their spell hits leave the target Exposed, taking 15% more damage from teammates for 3 seconds.
      • After a sleep, root, memory lock or suppression ends, the fighter shrugs off new hard control for 2.5 seconds.
      • There is no friendly fire. After 75 seconds, Overtime halves healing and adds 20% damage.
      • A team loses when all its fighters are down. At 120 seconds, team health %, then team damage, then a seeded coin toss decides.

      Formations: teams fight as a unit on a wide field. Tanks hold the front, damage dealers (mostly archers, gunners and casters) fight from behind them, and healers stay at the back within reach of the tanks. The team calls a focus target and peels for anyone who breaks into its back line. Melee damage dealers wait on a flank and dive with their mobility move when a target is exposed.

      Tactics set how a team picks targets and how risky its formation is: Hold the line keeps a tight block, Protect the carry guards its best damage dealer, Balanced is the default, Focus their healer hunts healers with flanking divers, and All-out aggression pushes fast with every melee fighter diving.

      Class icons: each fighter's circle shows what they fight with: a plus for healers, a hat for casters, a bow for rangers and gunners, and a shield for melee fighters.

      Maps: the open field or mirrored terrain (pillar hall, broken ruins, crossroads, stone groves). Terrain blocks movement, projectiles and line of sight, and fighters walk around it.

      Team leagues: found a league from the League tab. AI coaches draft rosters under a salary cap, then teams play a full season (10, 14 or 17 weeks) ranked by win %, head-to-head, division and conference record and HP margin. The playoffs are Bo3 rounds with re-seeding and a Bo5 Forgefire Crown final.

      Compositions and metas: no lineup is forced. Each coach has their own plan (fortress builders stack tanks, glass cannons field all damage, tacticians lean on control) and after every season coaches drift towards what won: tacticians copy the meta fast, fortress builders barely move. The offseason report shows the season's meta.

      Coach mode: use Coach a team in the league header before the draft or between seasons. You make your team's draft picks (only cap-legal picks are offered), set its starting lineup and tactic, and handle its offseason.

      Offseason: ratings move with each fighter's impact compared with others in the same role (at most ±5 per offseason, and gains above 90 are halved), and every contract is repriced. The cap follows the market. Coaches with dismal seasons are fired. Teams keep or release fighters and trade, and AI coaches only accept trades that make them better. Then a rookie class joins the free agents for a draft that runs worst record first, champion last. Free agents unsigned for two offseasons retire.

      Watched series pause between games. In your team league, choose starters and a tactic before continuing; returning to setup keeps the completed games. Expand Past champions for season winners.

      Team kits: new team recruits roll one extra support or control ability according to their role. Kits include area and chain healing, a one-use interruptible resurrection, cleanse, area wards, stun, slow, disarm, taunt and knock-up. Kits appear in fighter details and below the health bars. They do not change duel abilities.

      League patches: each league starts with base rules and can adjust its own combat balance at halfway and in the offseason. Changes require enough comparable games and stay within small limits. Expand League patches to read its history or freeze automatic adjustments. Replays keep the rules used for that game.

      Core siege preview: select it in 3v3 or 5v5 exhibitions or when founding a new league. Destroy the enemy crystal to win; two nearby defenders protect it, and downed fighters return at their base. At 6:00, respawns stop and Core damage doubles. The 6:30 limit compares Core health, then damage. The Forge Titan rises at 0:40. Its killing-blow team claims Forgefire for 60 seconds: stronger damage, a renewing shield, and a siege bonus that pierces Guarded. Watch its slam warning. Existing leagues keep their battle rules.
